Every week, an SME owner makes decisions that commit the company for years. Most of the time, they make them alone. Their employees depend on them, their family does not know the business, and their accountant mostly looks at the past. I lived that loneliness at Swiss Auto Glass. That is why I believe in the **advisory board for SMEs**: a small circle of outside specialists who challenge the boss, without taking their place.

This is not just a hunch. The figures exist.

## What the studies say

The most robust study on the subject comes from BDC, the Business Development Bank of Canada. It surveyed more than 1,000 SMEs and cross-referenced the answers with Statistics Canada tax data from 2001 to 2011 ([BDC via CNW](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/bdc-issues-first-ever-canadian-study-on-the-impact-of-advisory-boards-513960461.html)). The results:

- **+67% sales growth** on average in the three years after creating an advisory board, against +23% in the three years before.
- **Productivity growth that doubles**: +6% over three years, against +3% before.
- Over ten years, average sales **24% higher** and productivity **18% higher** than a comparison group.
- **86% of the business owners** concerned believe their board had a significant impact on their success.

The most telling figure is elsewhere: **only 6% of SMEs** have such a board. And among those that do not, 57% think it requires too much work, time and effort.

A study does not prove everything, of course. Companies that create a board may already be the ones that want to grow. But the before-and-after gap, within the same companies, is hard to ignore.

## An outside perspective, a question of survival

The issue is not just growth. It is also longevity. According to PwC, fewer than a third of family businesses make it to the second generation, and only 12% to the third ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/publications/documents/family-business-survey-2019.pdf)). The same survey shows that 84% of the business leaders surveyed in the Middle East plan to bring in outside professional expertise.

The companies that last are not the ones with the most brilliant boss. They are the ones where someone dares to ask the right questions before it is too late.

## What an advisory board really brings

An advisory board is not a board of directors. It decides nothing, carries no liability and does not replace the boss. That is precisely its strength. Its members are free to say what they think.

In practice, I see four benefits:

1. **Stepping back.** Leaving day-to-day operations four times a year to look at strategy changes the way you steer the rest of the year.
2. **Being contradicted.** A boss rarely hears "no" in their own company. An outside view, with no hierarchical stake, says it.
3. **Accessing skills you cannot hire.** Finance, HR, technology, market: an SME cannot afford a director for every area. A panel can provide them.
4. **Setting deadlines.** A scheduled session forces you to prepare your figures and report on what has been done. It is a discipline.

## How to set up an advisory board for your SME without overcomplicating things

The 57% who fear the burden are partly right: a poorly organised board wastes time. What I recommend:

- **Few members**, three at most, chosen for their skills and not for their address book.
- **An initial diagnosis** to know what to work on.
- **A fixed rhythm**, for example four sessions a year, prepared with the key figures.
- **Short, concrete sessions**, with decisions and follow-up.
- **A boss who remains the boss.** The board sheds light, the leader decides.

This is the model we have built with Patrick Grobéty at The Advisory Board since January 2025: one diagnostic session and four strategic sessions a year, a panel of specialists tailored to each company, and only one company per business sector. More than 120 entrepreneurs have already been supported by our panel.

## What I take from it

You do not win a rugby match alone, and you do not grow an SME alone either. A business leader does not need more opinions. They need a few competent, regular and honest perspectives. The figures show that it pays off. The hardest part is getting started.
