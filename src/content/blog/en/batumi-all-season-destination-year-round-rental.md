On 17 October, in Kotor, Montenegro, Batumi was named "Europe's Leading All-Season Destination" at the World Travel Awards for the fourth year running. It beat Salzburg, Geneva, Munich, Stockholm, Budapest, Oslo and Tallinn. For anyone looking at **Batumi real estate**, the award raises a real question: does the city actually live all year round, or only from July to September? I looked at the numbers, and the answer is more interesting than a simple yes.

## What the award says, and what it doesn't

A tourism award is not a yield indicator. But it is a sign of strategy. Since 2019, Batumi has been named "Europe's Leading Emerging Destination" five years in a row, then "All-Season Destination" four times. The city and the Adjara region have spent years stretching the season: conferences, festivals, the botanical garden in winter, the Goderdzi ski resort 115 km away, open roughly from December to April, and even the autumn migration of birds of prey, which draws enthusiasts from all over the world.

Visitor numbers follow. According to Geostat and the tourism administration, [as reported by International Investment](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/tourism/8976-batumi-receives-world-travel-awards-for-the-fourth-time.html), Adjara recorded 2.7 million international visits in 2025. In the first half of 2026 it counted 927,300, of which 621,300 in the second quarter. Batumi airport handled 906 flights in July 2026, 31% more than in July 2025, and nearly 3,000 international flights since the start of the year.

What the award doesn't say is that the Black Sea is still the Black Sea. Summer still dominates.

## Rental seasonality in Batumi, in figures

To measure the gap, I looked at short-term rental data from [AirROI](https://www.airroi.com/airbnb-data/georgia/autonomous-republic-of-adjara/batumi) for the twelve months from November 2025 to October 2026. It covers about 3,700 active listings in Batumi.

- **August**, the best month: 50.7% occupancy, an average of 196 lari a night, average revenue of 3,420 lari per listing.
- **December**, the weakest month: 30.6% occupancy, 155 lari a night, 1,330 lari per listing.
- **Over the year**: average occupancy of 36% and average revenue of 11,395 lari per listing, up 13.7% year on year.

In other words, an average flat earns about 2.6 times more in August than in December. That figure matters, but it has to be read correctly. It is an average across all listings, including badly located studios, poor photos and remote owners who are slow to answer. Another source, [Airbtics](https://airbtics.com/annual-airbnb-revenue-in-batumi-georgia/), gives a median occupancy of 54% over a similar period. The methods differ, and the gap mostly shows one thing: management makes the difference.

What reassures me is that winter doesn't drop to zero. In February, average occupancy climbs back to 38%, higher than in March or May. That is the direct result of the all-season strategy: skiing, weekends for Georgians from Tbilisi, business travellers and visitors from neighbouring countries, led by Turkey and Armenia, who come out of season.

## How I think about a property for year-round letting

For an investor, the question isn't whether Batumi is a summer destination. It is. The question is: how do you make the flat work for the other eight months? Here is what I look at with my clients.

**Location first.** In winter, travellers want the centre, the boulevard, restaurants that are open. A property by an isolated beach works very well in August and much less in January. Near old Batumi or the new boulevard, demand holds up better all year.

**A property built for winter.** Efficient heating, good insulation, quality windows, reliable internet for digital nomads. These are details at purchase, but they are what earn five-star reviews in December.

**A mixed rental model.** This is often the best answer to seasonality: short-term lets from May to October, then stays of one to six months in the low season, for expats, students or professionals on assignment. You give up a little on the nightly rate, but you gain occupancy and peace of mind.

**Professional on-site management.** Prices adjusted every week, good photos, fast replies to messages. That is what separates a flat at 30% winter occupancy from one at 50%.

## Investing in Batumi: the right reflexes

I remain positive about Batumi. The population has grown by 54% in ten years to more than 235,000. The city is attracting more and more flights and visitors, and Georgia keeps a simple tax framework: rental income for an individual can be taxed at 5%. But I always recommend three reflexes before buying:

1. **Calculate over twelve months, not over August.** A projection that multiplies the summer rent by twelve is wrong.
2. **Plan a cautious scenario.** Test your yield with 35% short-term occupancy, then compare it with a long-term let. If the investment holds up in both cases, it is a good investment.
3. **Choose the property for its tenant**, not for the view in the brochure. A three-bedroom for families and long stays and a well-located studio for solo travellers do not serve the same market.

The "all-season destination" title is good news for the market, because it confirms that the city is investing to fill the winter. It is up to the investor to make the most of it, with a property and management designed for all twelve months.
