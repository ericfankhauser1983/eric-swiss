In September 2026, 4,245 apartments changed hands in Tbilisi, for a total of $376 million. That is 6.4% more transactions than a year earlier, but above all 24.1% more value. These Colliers Georgia figures, reported by [FFI](https://www.francefinance.ae/articles-blog/tbilissi-ventes-appartements-septembre-2026-hausse-24-pourcent), answer the question almost all my clients ask me: is this still the right time for **buying an apartment in Tbilisi**? I took them apart, and they tell a more interesting story than a simple record.

## More value than volume: the market is moving upmarket

The gap between the two figures is the heart of the matter. When the number of sales grows by 6% and their value by 24%, people are paying more. According to calculations by [International Investment](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/real-estate/8970-georgias-property-market-apartment-sales-rise-in-tbilisi-fall-in-batumi.html) based on Colliers data, the average transaction reached about $88,600, up 16.6% year on year.

This rise has two causes, and they need to be kept apart.

- **Prices are rising.** Year on year, the weighted average price is up 11.9% for new builds sold by developers, 10% for resales of recent homes and 14% for older stock.
- **Buyers are going bigger and better located.** Part of the rise in the average value comes from the mix of properties sold, not only from prices: new builds and good neighbourhoods account for a growing share.

This is not a one-off. In August, Tbilisi recorded 3,388 sales worth $297 million, up 35% in value. Over the whole of 2025, Colliers counted 42,267 sales worth $3.3 billion, according to [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/tbilisi-and-batumi-apartment-sales-surpass-4-3-billion-in-2025/). The trend is solid and lasting.

I also note that volume growth is slowing: +22.8% in July, +18.9% in August, +6.4% in September. I don't see this as a warning. A market that shifts from very fast growth in the number of sales to growth through quality is a market that is maturing.

## New builds drive the Tbilisi market

Sales in new developments are up 9.8% year on year. In detail, developers' direct sales gained 6.3% and resales of recent homes 14.6%. Older stock fell 4.7%.

That resale figure matters a lot to me. Resales in new builds prove there is liquidity: owners who bought a few years ago are finding buyers. For an investor, this is essential. You don't only make money by buying well, you make money by being able to sell.

New-build prices are rising everywhere, but not at the same pace. According to the same data, they are up 12.3% in the centre, 15% in adjacent districts and 9.9% on the outskirts. The intermediate areas, between the centre and the outskirts, are benefiting most from demand. In 2025, Didi Dighomi, Saburtalo and Samgori alone accounted for 61% of supply and demand. That is where the everyday market plays out.

## Foreign buyers: 12% of sales, and that's good news

In September, foreign buyers accounted for 12% of transactions in Tbilisi, compared with 14% in August. In Batumi, the share is 48%.

You could see this as a weakness. I see it as a strength. In Tbilisi, the market rests first on local demand: Georgians buying a home, families trading up, a middle class growing with a fast-expanding economy. That demand is less volatile than foreign investors, who come and go with the cycles. For someone buying to rent out all year, it is a reassuring foundation: your tenants are there, and they don't depend on the tourist season.

In 2025, 86% of foreign purchases in Tbilisi were in new developments. Foreigners follow the same logic as the local market, with an even stronger preference for recent buildings.

## Buying an apartment in Tbilisi: my method

The numbers are good. All the more reason to buy with method. Here is what I look at with my clients.

**The neighbourhood before the price per square metre.** A slightly more expensive property in a district that rents well earns more than a bargain on the outskirts that nobody wants. In my view, the districts adjacent to the centre currently offer the best balance between entry price, rental demand and potential for appreciation.

**The developer before the brochure.** With new builds, everything depends on the developer's ability to deliver, on time and at the promised quality. I look at their completed projects, their actual delivery times and the condition of their buildings a few years later.

**Yield calculated net.** Rent received by an individual can be taxed at 5% in Georgia. That is a real advantage, but you also have to count management, service charges, furnishing and vacancy periods. I always run a cautious scenario before talking about yield.

**Residency as a bonus.** Since March 2026, according to [industry professionals](https://gpi-realty.com.ge/en/blog/66z12cpen1-residence-permit-in-georgia-through-real), buying property worth at least $150,000 lets you apply for a temporary residence permit. From $300,000, the investor residence permit is valid for five years. I always have the current conditions checked with the authorities before signing.

## My takeaway

Tbilisi is no longer a market for pioneers. It is a market rising in value, where new builds dominate, resales work and local demand remains the engine. That makes selection more demanding, but the investment more solid.
