"+137%", "+165%", "+186%". When you look into **real estate investment in Georgia**, you quickly come across figures like these. And the question I am asked most often is simple: do you **really make capital gains in Georgia**, or is it a sales pitch to sell off-plan apartments?

My answer: the gains are real, but you need to know how to read them. I took three concrete cases from Archi, one of the largest developers in Tbilisi: a completed project, a project under construction and an actual resale.

## What the developer's figures say

Archi publishes how the price per m² of its projects has moved, from the launch price to today's selling price. Two examples stand out for me.

**Archi Central Park**, a 29-storey building near Tbilisi's central park, is **completed**. Launched in January 2019 at **$1,180/m²**, it now sells at around **$2,800/m²**. That is +137% in just under eight years, or roughly **11.8% a year**.

**Archi Universe**, in the Vake district, will be **completed in 2027**. Launched in April 2024 at **$1,565/m²**, it is now listed at **$2,600/m²**. That is +66% in two and a half years, or roughly **22.5% a year**, and the building isn't even finished.

![Price per m² at launch and in 2026: Archi Central Park, Archi Universe and the Grand Avenue client case](/images/blog/plus-values-georgie/prix-m2-en.svg)

## How to read these figures without fooling yourself

I am an entrepreneur, not a salesman. So I look at these figures with three questions.

**Where does the figure come from?** These are the developer's data. The 2026 price is what Archi sells at today, not necessarily what a private owner gets on the secondary market. For instance, the Korter portal lists Archi Universe "from $2,162/m²". The gap depends on floor, view and size.

**What should you compare it with?** According to [Galt & Taggart](https://ramad.bog.ge/s3/galt/mbmsFtZb-Tbilisi-Residential-Real-Estate_ENG_Dec-2025.pdf), the average price of new builds in Tbilisi rose from $1,233/m² in 2023 to $1,373/m² in 2025. That is about 5.5% a year. Solid, but far from the 12 to 22% of the Archi projects. The Vake average is $2,781/m², so Archi Universe is still below the average of its own district.

**Why such a gap?** Because the launch price of an off-plan project is an entry price. The developer sells cheaper to the first buyers, who take on the construction risk. Then the price rises at each stage: structure, façade, handover. That is where the gain is made, and that is why **your entry point matters more than anything else**.

![Average annual capital growth: Archi projects compared with Tbilisi's new-build market](/images/blog/plus-values-georgie/croissance-annuelle-en.svg)

My conclusion: it isn't marketing, but it isn't the whole market either. These gains are made on well-located projects, bought early, from a solid developer. An ordinary apartment bought at the wrong time will rather follow the average.

## A real case: +39.8% in 13 months at Grand Avenue

Archi also publishes resale cases. This one speaks volumes.

On 20 September 2025, an investor (D. L.) bought off-plan a **60.4 m²** one-bedroom apartment on a high floor in **Grand Avenue by Archi**. Price: **$97,224**, or about $1,610/m².

Thirteen months later, he resold it for **$135,898**, or about $2,250/m². His capital gain: **$38,674**, i.e. **+39.8% in 13 months**. The building will only be completed in 2029.

Two points to be complete. This figure is gross: it does not account for resale costs or tax. In Georgia, the gain on a home is only tax-exempt after two years of ownership. And it is a successful case, chosen by the developer. It shows what is possible, not what is guaranteed.

## What if he had not paid everything at signing?

What interests me most in this story is a detail: this client chose to **pay 100% at signing**. He tied up $97,224 to earn $38,674. His return on equity is 39.8%.

Yet Archi offers **interest-free** payment plans during construction, with a down payment starting at **20%**. Let's take a simple assumption: 20% down at signing, i.e. $19,445, then the balance in 28 monthly instalments of about $2,778.

After 13 months, he would have paid about **$55,557**. On resale, the buyer takes over the remaining instalments. The gain stays the same, $38,674, but it is earned with much less money. His return on equity rises to **about 69.6%**. That is almost double, and the $41,667 not committed could have been working elsewhere.

![Return on equity: cash payment compared with 20% down and instalments](/images/blog/plus-values-georgie/fonds-propres-en.svg)

This is exactly the principle I explain in my article on [return on equity](/en/blog/return-on-equity-leverage-real-estate-georgia/). The gain is calculated on the whole apartment, but you have only financed part of it. And here, the "leverage" costs no interest.

An honest caveat: developers often give a discount to buyers who pay cash. So compare both prices before choosing. And make sure you can pay every instalment to the end, even if the resale takes longer than planned.

## My rules before buying off-plan in Georgia

- **Get in early**, at launch or during the structural works: that is where most of the gain is made.
- **Choose the developer before the apartment**: delivery track record, completed projects, real quality.
- **Compare with the district average**, not only with the developer's figures.
- **Use the interest-free payment plan** when the gap with the cash price is small, while keeping a safety reserve.
- **Aim for at least two years** of ownership to benefit from the capital gains exemption.

So, reality or sales pitch? Capital gains are very real in Georgia, but they are built: the right project, the right timing, the right payment structure.
