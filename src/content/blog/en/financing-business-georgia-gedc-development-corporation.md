On 20 October, the Georgian government presented the Georgia Economic Development Corporation, the GEDC. Its stated goal is to support up to 40 billion lari of investment over ten years, roughly 15 billion dollars. In the days that followed, I received several messages asking the same question: "Will this push property prices up?"

Before answering, I wanted to know what this corporation really is. So I read the official announcements and the Georgian business press. My conclusion is simple: **the GEDC is not a real estate programme**. It is a tool for **financing a business in Georgia**. For an entrepreneur who wants to set up here, it is better news than it is for someone buying an apartment.

## What the GEDC really is

Prime Minister Irakli Kobakhidze announced its creation on 8 April 2026 at a cabinet meeting, according to [Business Insider Georgia](https://businessinsider.ge/Politics/pm-announces-establishment-of-georgia-s-new-economic-development-corporation-and-independent-investment-agency-34674?lng=eng). On 18 June, Economy Minister Mariam Kvrivishvili introduced its director general, Irakli Gabriadze. The [ministry](https://economy.ge/?lang=en&nw=3121&page=news) sums up the mission in one sentence: **improve access to finance for the private sector**.

In practice, the GEDC takes over the Enterprise Georgia agency and absorbs the functions of the Georgian Development Fund, the former Partnership Fund. It no longer handles the promotion of foreign investment: that job moves to an independent investment agency, recreated within the ministry. Such an agency existed until 2017, as [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgia-to-create-economic-development-corporation-and-separate-investment-agency-pm-says/) points out.

I like this split. On one side, a body that finances. On the other, a body that welcomes the investor. It is clearer than one desk that does everything halfway.

## Real estate or investment in general?

That was my first question. None of the official announcements I read lists housing or residential development among the priorities. The sectors highlighted are **local manufacturing**, to reduce dependence on imports, then **energy**, named as the pilot sector, and finally **logistics and infrastructure**, according to Deputy Minister Irakli Nadareishvili quoted by [BM.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/economic-development-corporation-to-support-gel-40-bln-in-projects-over-10-years-gabriadze).

The closest link to bricks and mortar is hospitality. The Enterprise Georgia programme co-financed 136 hotel projects, more than 3,800 rooms, **outside Tbilisi and Batumi**, according to figures published by [FFI](https://www.francefinance.ae/articles-blog/georgie-corporation-developpement-economique-40-milliards-lari). The aim was to help operators build a business, not to subsidise apartment purchases.

The effect on real estate exists, but it is indirect. Factories, power plants and warehouses create jobs and wages. Higher wages raise demand for housing, first for rentals, then for purchases. That is healthy, but slow. Nobody should buy an apartment in Tbilisi because a public agency announces 40 billion. You buy for the yield and the quality of the project. For that, see my page on [real estate investment in Georgia](/en/real-estate-investment-georgia/).

## The tools announced and the figures

Enterprise Georgia did not lend directly: it mostly subsidised interest rates on bank loans. The GEDC widens the toolbox. According to [InternationalInvestment.biz](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/business/8990-georgia-establishes-economic-development-corporation-to-support-business-and-investment.html), it plans direct loans, equity stakes, project co-financing, investments in funds and a factoring platform.

According to the breakdown presented by FFI, the 40 billion lari split as follows:

- 16 billion through existing programmes;
- 7 billion through new instruments;
- 16 billion through participation in securities issues;
- 1 billion in private equity funds.

To measure the ambition, look at the past. From 2014 to 2025, Enterprise Georgia supported more than 20,000 entrepreneurs and backed 8.5 billion lari of investment. Reaching 40 billion in ten years means almost multiplying the pace by five. The IMF has warned of two risks, according to BM.ge: competition with commercial banks and the cost to the budget.

Context matters too. Foreign direct investment fell 22% in the first half of 2026, to 750 million dollars, according to figures cited by FFI. The state wants to take over part of the effort. The intention is good. The execution remains to be proven.

## What I take from it for an entrepreneur setting up here

In Switzerland, an SME owner looking for financing almost always goes through their bank, and the bank asks for collateral. Here, the state is giving itself the means to share part of the risk. For a productive project, that is a real opportunity. I see three practical consequences.

**1. Structure the project so it can be financed.** The instruments target manufacturing, energy, logistics and exports, not trading or pure services. If your project creates value in Georgia, present it that way from day one.

**2. Have a clean, up-to-date Georgian company.** No programme will finance a structure without reliable accounts. It is all the easier because reinvested profit is not taxed, as I explain in my article on [business taxation in Georgia](/en/blog/business-taxation-georgia-estonian-model/).

**3. Check the conditions before building a business plan on them.** To date, the detailed criteria of the new instruments have not been published. I would only count on a public loan or equity stake once the conditions are in writing and the application has been accepted. A subsidy is a bonus. It does not replace a business model.

## My view

The GEDC is a positive signal. Georgia has understood that it cannot rely only on real estate and tourism. It wants a productive base: energy, factories, ports. That is exactly what makes a country solid over twenty years, and it is also what will, in time, support property values.
