From July to September 2026, Georgia welcomed 2,131,908 tourist stays with at least one overnight. That is 4.7% more than a year earlier and the best quarter ever recorded, according to the Georgian National Tourism Administration, figures reported by [FFI](https://www.francefinance.ae/articles-blog/georgie-tourisme-trimestre-record-2-13-millions). The record makes a good headline. But for anyone who wants to **invest in Georgia** and rent out an apartment in Batumi or Tbilisi, it is not the most important number. What matters is how long visitors stay, where they come from and how much they spend.

## A record in value more than in volume

Over nine months, Georgia counted 4.40 million tourist stays, up 2.3%. In total international visits, including day trips, the country is flat: 6.11 million, almost the same as in 2025, according to [International Investment](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/tourism/8980-tourism-in-georgia-in-2026-record-third-quarter.html).

Revenue, however, is accelerating sharply. International tourism brought in USD 1.86 billion in the third quarter, 11.9% more than a year earlier, according to the National Bank of Georgia. Revenue is growing more than twice as fast as stays.

The reading is the same as for the Tbilisi property market: the market is no longer growing only in numbers, it is moving upmarket. Fewer same-day trips, more tourists sleeping over: overnight stays reached 72.1% of visits over nine months, against 70.5% a year earlier.

## 6.4 nights on average: the figure I look at first

According to Geostat, a foreign visitor stayed 6.44 nights on average in the third quarter of 2026, 2.2% more than a year earlier. Over the whole of 2025, the average was 5.63 nights, against 4.11 in 2019. In six years, stays have become about 37% longer.

For an owner, that is excellent news, for three reasons.

- **Fewer turnovers.** A one-week stay instead of three nights means half as many cleanings, key handovers and platform fees for the same number of nights rented.
- **More stable occupancy.** Longer stays fill the midweek gaps that weigh on the yield of very short rentals.
- **A door to mid-term rentals.** A visitor who stays a week on holiday sometimes comes back for a month to work remotely. That is the clientele that keeps an apartment busy outside summer.

## Europeans are coming, and spending more

The second signal concerns where visitors come from. Over nine months, the European Union and the United Kingdom accounted for 473,620 visits, up 20.1%, with growth from all 27 EU countries. Their spending reached USD 665 million, up 29.1%, according to the National Bank.

In the third quarter, the strongest increases came from Denmark (+56.5%), Lithuania (+46.8%), Cyprus (+45.2%), Italy (+22.7%), the Netherlands (+20.1%), the United Kingdom (+18.2%) and Germany (+16.8%). China rose 30.2% and Japan 24.5%.

For an investor, this diversification matters as much as growth. A rental market that depends on two or three source countries is fragile: one crisis, devaluation or border closure is enough. A market that attracts Europeans, Gulf visitors, Israelis, Turks and Asians at the same time is far more resilient.

## What it means for renting in Batumi and Tbilisi

Batumi remains the country's second destination after Tbilisi. The head of the Adjara government reports more than 2 million visitors from January to July, and several hotels exceeded 95% occupancy in July and August. Batumi airport passed one million passengers as early as September. In a [recent article](/en/blog/batumi-all-season-destination-year-round-rental/) I explained how I handle the low season.

In practice, this is how I adjust my reading of a rental investment to these figures.

1. **I calculate yield on a realistic average stay**, around five to seven nights, not on isolated nights. That changes the cleaning and management cost calculation.
2. **I favour apartments a European would choose for a week or a month**: a real work corner, reliable internet, an equipped kitchen, walking distance to the sea or the centre.
3. **In Tbilisi, I look at business and remote-work guests**, who are less seasonal than Batumi's.
4. **I stay cautious about promised yields.** Visitor figures are official. A net yield printed in a brochure is not. I always redo the calculation with real costs: management, cleaning, tax, vacancy.

## My view

The third-quarter record does not mean every apartment in Batumi will be profitable. It says that tourist demand in Georgia is changing in nature: more visitors sleeping over, staying longer, coming from further away and spending more. That is exactly the guest profile that makes a rental more stable and easier to manage.
