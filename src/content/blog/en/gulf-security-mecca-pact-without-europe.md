On Thursday 26 November, two Italian technicians from Rheinmetall Italia were killed in Kuwait by a drone attack while servicing an air defence system. Kuwait's defence ministry called it an Iranian attack. Tehran denied any operation in the region. The same day, a strike attributed to the Houthis killed a worker at Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery and forced a temporary shutdown of part of the site.

Europe's reaction came that evening, on X. Ursula von der Leyen called for "stepping up pressure on Iran" and said that "security in the Gulf is a matter of security for us all", according to [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/middle-east-conflict-saudi-refinery-attack-kills-worker-iran-denies-deadly-kuwait-strike-1.500761343)'s live coverage. Seen from Tbilisi, what strikes me is less the statement than what was happening in the region at the same time: Gulf security is being built, but Europe is not at the table.

## What Europe looks at, and what it doesn't

In the European reading, the subject is Iran: sanctions, pressure, isolation. That is a legitimate reading. But it leaves out how the countries of the region are organising themselves.

On the same day, Pakistan's defence minister Khawaja Asif told AFP that the Mecca pact countries were due to meet in Islamabad to discuss the conflict between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis, with the Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani defence and foreign ministers. On the Iranian side, Tehran said that Qatari and Pakistani mediators were continuing their efforts, according to [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/mena/us-israel-iran-saudi-arabia-yemen-war-live-updates-november-26-2026).

In other words, security is discussed in Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad, mediation runs through Doha and Islamabad, and Washington negotiates directly: US envoy Jared Kushner says he has met Iranian representatives several times. He added, in passing, that European countries "ask a lot of America".

## The Mecca pact: an architecture built in three months

This pact is still little known in Europe. Yet it took shape very quickly.

- **7 August 2026:** Mohammed bin Salman, Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Shehbaz Sharif sign a trilateral defence agreement in Mecca. According to Pakistan's foreign ministry, quoted by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/saudi-%E2%81%A0arabia-pakistan-and-turkiye-sign-defence-deal-amid-regional-turmoil), an attack on any member will be regarded as an attack on all.
- **31 August:** first meeting in Istanbul. The three countries decide to set up a permanent secretariat in Riyadh.
- **5 October:** emergency meeting in Riyadh after attacks on Saudi cities. A first Pakistani secretary general is appointed for three years.
- **7 November:** the Turkish parliament ratifies the agreement by 279 votes to 119, according to [The Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2639516/makkah-defence-alliance-set-for-islamabad-huddle).

In three months, Saudi Arabia, NATO's second-largest army and the only nuclear power in the Muslim world have given themselves a common framework. You can think what you like about it. But it is a fact: the Gulf no longer delegates its security entirely to the West.

## Why it matters from Tbilisi

Turkey is our direct neighbour and one of Georgia's leading trading partners. When Ankara becomes a guarantor of Saudi security, the Caucasus finds itself at the junction of two architectures: NATO, of which Turkey is a member, and the Mecca pact.

Gulf capital is already here. In the second quarter of 2026, the United Arab Emirates was the third-largest foreign investor in Georgia, with USD 47.7 million, behind China and the United Kingdom, according to preliminary Geostat data reported by [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/china-was-georgias-largest-foreign-investor-in-q2-2026-geostat/). A quarter of the quarter's direct investment went into real estate.

For a Gulf investor, Georgia offers what is missing at home today: a country away from the conflict, linked by road to Turkey and by air to the major Gulf cities. The more the region organises itself for a long conflict, the more valuable this role as a rear base becomes.

## What I take from it as an investor

I am neither a soldier nor a diplomat. I look at money flows, and this is what they tell me.

- **Oil remains under pressure.** Brent was trading at USD 94.71 on the morning of 26 November, after a 3.5% rise the day before, according to Gulf News. Ships are still being hit near Hormuz. Any business plan that depends on energy or freight must factor in this level, not the pre-war one.
- **Decisions are made in the region.** To understand where Gulf money will go, you have to listen to Riyadh, Doha, Abu Dhabi, Ankara and Islamabad, not only Brussels and Washington.
- **Geographic diversification is no longer theory.** Gulf families and companies are already applying it. Wealth concentrated in a single region, however prosperous, carries a risk that 2026 has made visible.
- **Georgia has a card to play, provided it stays neutral and predictable.** Its appeal to Gulf capital rests on stability and clear rules. That is what to watch, far more than press releases.

Europe is right to want an end to attacks on its partners. But as long as it limits itself to calling for "more pressure", others are writing the rules. For an entrepreneur, the lesson is simple: go where decisions are made.
