On 13 November, an IMF mission ended a five-day visit to Tbilisi. Its conclusion: Georgia should grow by **7.2% in 2026**. In April, the same IMF expected 5.3%. If you want to **invest in Georgia**, this figure deserves more than a headline. I read the statement in full, with its good news and its warnings. Here is what I took from it.

## What the IMF says, figure by figure

The mission, led by Alejandro Hajdenberg from 9 to 13 November, paints a solid picture ([IMF statement, reprinted by Mirage News](https://www.miragenews.com/imf-staff-conclude-georgia-visit-1777109/)):

- **Growth**: 7.4% year on year from January to September 2026, after 7.5% in 2025. Forecast of 7.2% for 2026 and 5.7% for 2027, before converging towards a potential of around 5% a year.
- **Public debt**: expected to fall to about 33% of GDP.
- **Fiscal deficit**: about 1.5% of GDP in 2026. The draft 2027 budget targets 2.5%.
- **Foreign exchange reserves**: a record $8.2 billion at the end of October.
- **Current account**: the deficit narrowed to 2.5% of GDP in the first half of the year.

For comparison, France's public debt stood at 119% of GDP at the end of June 2026, according to Insee as quoted by FFI. This is not the same category of risk.

## A country the forecasts underestimate

What strikes me most is not the 7.2%. It is the repeated gap between what institutions announce and what Georgia delivers. In April, during its annual mission, the IMF expected 5.3% for 2026 ([OC Media](https://oc-media.org/imf-sees-continued-economic-growth-for-georgia-in-2026-barring-iran-war-escalation)). It has revised by almost two points in seven months. FFI points out that the same pattern played out in previous years: 5.7% forecast in April 2024 against more than 9% achieved, around 6% forecast in early 2025 against 7.5% achieved ([FFI](https://www.francefinance.ae/articles-blog/georgie-fmi-croissance-7-2-dette-33-pourcent)).

Why? Cautious models assume a return to the mean. Georgia, meanwhile, benefits from flows they capture poorly: Middle Corridor transit, tourism, and the capital and entrepreneurs who settle here. I am not telling you this outperformance will last forever. The IMF itself says growth "has moderated somewhat in recent months". But a market that has beaten its forecasts for three years is not an exotic bet. It is a trend with a track record.

## What it changes for real estate

The IMF mentions real estate itself. In its view, stronger investment, "including from large real estate projects", could further support activity. For a buyer, I see three concrete effects.

1. **Rental demand follows growth.** More activity means more managers, entrepreneurs and business travellers to house in Tbilisi and Batumi.
2. **Fiscal stability protects the tax framework.** A state with little debt does not need to look for new revenue from property owners. In Georgia, individuals' rental income is taxed at 5%. I watch this closely, because it is what drives net yield.
3. **Record reserves support the lari.** For an investor who thinks in francs or euros, the strength of the currency matters as much as the yield in local currency.

## The warnings I do not hide

A good investor also reads the uncomfortable paragraphs. The IMF gives three.

- **Inflation** reached 5.9% year on year in October, above the National Bank's 3% target. The policy rate is 8.25% and the IMF believes a "tighter" stance may be needed. Local credit will therefore stay expensive.
- **Dollarisation** remains a weakness. The IMF calls for monitoring the rise in foreign-currency lending, including mortgages. My advice is simple: do not borrow in a currency you do not earn.
- **External risks** exist: renewed escalation in the Middle East, political tensions and the relationship with the European Union. An end to the war in Ukraine could also reverse some of the migration and financial flows that have arrived since 2022.

None of these points stops me from investing. They force me to be selective. I favour projects financed with equity or a solid down payment, developers who deliver, and locations that rent all year round, not just in summer.

## How I use this figure

An IMF forecast is not a sales pitch. It is a framework. It tells me Georgia has fiscal room, reserves and growth above its European neighbours, with inflation to watch. That is exactly the context in which I want to buy: methodically, without excessive leverage, and by calculating net yield rather than advertised yield.
