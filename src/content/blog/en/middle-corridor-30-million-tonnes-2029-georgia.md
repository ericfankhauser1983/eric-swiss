On 19 November in Shenzhen, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin signed a four-point "peace declaration". One point pledges to increase the supply of refined petroleum products and stresses the importance of "stable energy flows". According to Reuters, the text says nothing about the war with Iran. It does not mention Hormuz either, although the strait is still closed ([Reuters, via Club of Mozambique](https://clubofmozambique.com/news/trump-xi-and-putin-release-peace-declaration-from-unprecedented-summit/); [NTD](https://www.ntd.com/trump-xi-putin-agree-to-peace-declaration-pledge-nuclear-risk-talks_1186323.html)). Three days earlier in Tbilisi, a Georgian minister had announced a concrete target for the **Middle Corridor**. Seen from here, the contrast says a lot. The great powers talk about stability. The region is building the routes that make it possible.

## What Tbilisi announced

On 16 November, Deputy Prime Minister and Economy Minister Mariam Kvrivishvili presented on Imedi TV a plan developed "in coordination" with Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan. The goal: **30 million tonnes of cargo through the Middle Corridor by 2029** ([APA](https://en.apa.az/foreign-policy/georgia-azerbaijan-and-kazakhstan-develop-joint-plan-for-middle-corridor-development-536269)). One caveat: so far only the Georgian side has presented this plan publicly. I have not found an equivalent statement from Astana or Baku.

The next day, her deputy Gennadi Arveladze set out the means on Rustavi 2. He aims to double Georgia's cargo and logistics capacity on the corridor. The country plans to buy more than 2,000 freight wagons in two to three years. It has already bought 400, of which 200 have been delivered. Georgian ports handled 17.1 million tonnes in the first ten months of 2026, the best figure in a decade, and rail carried 12 million tonnes ([Report.az](https://report.az/en/region/georgia-along-with-azerbaijan-kazakhstan-to-boot-middle-corridor-cargo-capacity)).

## An ambitious target, figures to read carefully

I prefer to look at the numbers squarely. According to official Kazakh data, the Trans-Caspian route (TITR) carried 2.76 million tonnes in 2023, then 4.5 million in 2024 and 4.1 million in 2025, a drop of 8.9%. Container numbers still rose by 36%, to 77,000 TEU. Kazakhstan's Ministry of Transport puts current capacity at about 6 million tonnes a year ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2026/10/can-middle-corridor-deliver-on-its-promise/)).

The 30-million-tonne target therefore probably covers a wider scope than Trans-Caspian traffic alone: all east–west freight crossing Georgia. The ministry did not say. But the order of magnitude shows the ambition. A researcher at Heriot-Watt University puts it well: the corridor is "maturing politically faster than it is maturing operationally".

Operational progress is real, though. Transit time has fallen from 38 days to 13 to 17 days, and the target is now 10 to 12 days from China to the ports of Georgia and Türkiye. Customs clearance has gone from eight hours to 30 minutes ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2026/10/can-middle-corridor-deliver-on-its-promise/)).

## Who is really paying for the route

This is where the usual European reading seems incomplete to me. In Brussels, the Middle Corridor is often presented as a European "connectivity" project. The amounts tell another story.

- The **World Bank** provides an $846 million guarantee. It is meant to mobilise $1.41 billion of financing for a 322-kilometre railway line in Kazakhstan.
- The **EBRD** lends €35 million to rebuild two berths at the port of Aktau.
- The **European Union** plans a grant of up to €10 million for the same project.
- On the **Chinese** side, Lianyungang Port built the Aktau container hub. China Railway Container Transport joined the corridor's multimodal consortium in January ([The Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2026/10/can-middle-corridor-deliver-on-its-promise/)).

I am not saying Europe is absent. I am saying that operators, development banks and Asian investors are positioning themselves on this route faster than Europe is. Yet the goods it carries are heading for Europe.

## Why Iran makes this route essential

As long as Hormuz stays closed and the war with Iran has no exit framework, the routes that bypass Iran to the north gain value. The Shenzhen declaration changes nothing. It promises fuel, not the reopening of a strait. For Beijing, Astana and Baku, the corridor through Georgia is insurance against blockages, and they are paying for it themselves.

At the same time, the regional energy map is shifting. On 18 November, the Azerbaijani and Georgian energy ministers reviewed their cooperation. In the first eight months of 2026, Azerbaijan's electricity exports to Russia, Georgia and Iran fell 3.2-fold in value year on year ([Trend](https://www.trend.az/azerbaijan/4247673.html)). The region is reorganising its flows without waiting for the big capitals.

## What I take from this as an entrepreneur and investor

1. **Logistics is the first winner.** More wagons and ports at record levels mean demand for warehouses, forwarders, maintenance and services around Poti, Batumi and Tbilisi.
2. **Business services follow.** Accounting, recruitment, insurance, customs advice: every foreign company that sets up to manage its flows needs a local ecosystem.
3. **Keep an eye on delivery.** The 2029 target is political. I will track wagon deliveries, quarterly volumes and real transit times before treating it as a certainty.
