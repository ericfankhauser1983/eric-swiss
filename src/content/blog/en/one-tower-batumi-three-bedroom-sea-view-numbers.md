When an investor asks me about **Batumi real estate**, the first question is almost always the same: "What does it return?" That is the wrong first question. The right one is: "How much do I have to put in, when, and what happens if the assumptions don't hold?" Here I take a real case, a three-bedroom apartment at One Tower, and I cost it line by line, the way I do with my clients.

## The project in a few facts

One Tower is a residential tower of 483 apartments on 33 floors in central Batumi, on Tbel Abuseridze street. The developer, ONE Development, has delivered or launched five projects totalling about 415,000 m². Delivery is scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2027.

Two technical points reassured me. The pile foundations go down 42 metres, and the structure is designed for a magnitude 8 earthquake. On the Adjara coast, that is not a detail.

The project has another feature. It is the first one launched under the municipal "Batumi without dangerous buildings" programme. Started in 2022 by the city and the Adjara government, it targets 118 run-down buildings where about 3,000 families live. In practice, a risky building is demolished and rebuilt, and the residents are rehoused. For a buyer, it signals that the project has the backing of the local authorities.

Underlying demand is there too. According to the 2024 census, Batumi has more than 235,000 inhabitants, 54% more than in 2014.

## The payment plan, line by line

Take a three-bedroom of 119.8 m² with a sea view, priced at $326,722 with finishing, or about €286,600. Without finishing, it is $256,372.

Here is how the money goes out:

- **At booking:** 30%, about €86,000, plus about €5,000 in fees.
- **During construction:** about €43,000 spread over 14 months.
- **At delivery:** a balance of about €157,600, financed by a 10-year loan at about €1,870 a month.

The number to remember is not the price. It is the **cash outlay before delivery: about €134,000**. That money is tied up, with no rent coming in, for more than a year. Before signing, I always ask clients whether they can leave that sum locked up until the end of 2027, even if something unexpected happens elsewhere.

On financing, the project is 100% financed by TBC Bank. For our clients, we generally obtain rates of 7 to 8%. The €1,870 monthly payment corresponds to a rate of about 7.5%. That is more expensive than a Swiss mortgage, which is exactly why the calculation has to stand on the rent, not on hope.

## What the apartment can earn

Rent estimates are €21,600 to €23,700 a year for long-term letting, and €26,850 to €32,340 a year for short-term letting. These are gross figures.

Against a price of €286,600, that is a gross yield of 7.5 to 8.3% long term. Short term, the gross yield rises to 9 to 11%, but you have to deduct management, cleaning, platform fees and seasonality, which remains marked in Batumi. The low season can be managed. It cannot be ignored.

What I look at most is the balance. Twelve payments of €1,870 come to €22,440 a year. Long-term rent therefore roughly covers the loan. In other words, once delivered, the apartment pays for itself, and the investor keeps the €134,000 contribution as equity. That **leverage effect** is what makes the difference. It is also where you need to stay careful: if the rent drops or the unit sits empty for three months, you pay the instalment.

On tax, the Georgian framework remains simple. An individual's rental income can be taxed at 5%, and the capital gain is exempt if the property is held for more than two years. Each situation still needs to be checked against your country of residence.

## The assumptions I don't take for granted

The FFI projection assumes appreciation of 6% a year. On that basis, the property would be worth about €383,500 in five years and €513,000 in ten. It is a reasonable assumption given the market's track record, but it is **not a promise**, and it is not contractual.

This is what I would check before booking:

- **The delivery schedule.** A six-month delay means six more months without rent, with your contribution tied up.
- **The final loan rate.** One extra percentage point adds roughly €80 to the monthly payment.
- **The type of letting.** Long term is more stable. Short term earns more but needs serious management.
- **The choice of a three-bedroom.** We recommend it because it targets families and long stays, a segment with less competition than studios.
