When I talk about investing with my clients, one question almost always comes up: "How much does it return?" And almost always, the person is thinking of the property's yield: the rent divided by the price. It is a good start, but it is not the figure that really matters.

The figure that matters is the **return on equity**. In other words: how much does the money that **you** actually took out of your pocket earn? Once you understand this nuance, you never look at **real estate investment in Georgia** the same way again.

## The property's return and the return on your money

The property's yield measures how the apartment performs. The return on equity measures how your money performs. They are not the same thing.

An image I like: when a company invests in a new machine, its boss does not just ask what the machine earns. He asks what each franc he put in himself earns. If the bank financed part of the machine, the answer changes everything. During my years at the head of Swiss Auto Glass, that was always the right question: "how much does my money earn?"

In real estate, the principle is the same. And the tool that makes the difference is called **leverage**.

## Leverage, in one sentence

You buy a $100,000 property while putting in only $30,000. The bank finances the rest. But you are the one who collects **100% of the rent and 100% of the capital gain**.

You only pay the bank interest on the money it lends you. Everything else is yours, and it is earned on a stake three times smaller.

## A concrete example in Tbilisi

Take a $100,000 apartment in Tbilisi. Over one year, it produces two things.

**Rent.** The gross rental yield in Tbilisi reaches 8.6% according to [Galt & Taggart](https://www.gbc.ge/en/news/Economics-news/galttaggart-predicts-single-digit-growth-in-apartment-prices-in-tbilisi-in-2026). Residential rent is taxed at only 5%, as [FFI](https://www.francefinance.ae/articles-blog/conference-paris-100-personnes-immobilier-georgie) points out. So I assume $8,000 of net rent a year.

**Capital gain.** Prices per m² in Tbilisi rose 4% over one year according to [TBC Capital](https://tbccapital.ge/static/file/202604225003-tbilisi-residential-market-monthly-watch-march-2026-eng.pdf). I assume 4%, or $4,000 of added value.

**If you pay cash**, you put in $100,000. You earn $8,000 in rent and $4,000 in capital gain, or $12,000. Your return on equity is **12%**. That is already very good.

**If you use leverage**, you put in $30,000 and the bank lends $70,000. With a loan at 7% a year, the assumption used in our simulator, you pay around $4,740 in interest in the first year. The property still earns you $8,000 in rent and $4,000 in capital gain. So you are left with $7,260.

$7,260 on a $30,000 stake is a **24% return on equity**. The same apartment, the same rent, the same capital gain. But the return on your money has doubled.

| | Cash | With leverage |
|---|---|---|
| Your stake | $100,000 | $30,000 |
| Net rent | $8,000 | $8,000 |
| Capital gain (+4%) | $4,000 | $4,000 |
| Loan interest | — | −$4,740 |
| **Your gain** | **$12,000** | **$7,260** |
| **Return on equity** | **12%** | **24%** |

## Why it works: the capital gain applies to the whole property

That is the secret. When the property gains 4% in value, it gains 4% on $100,000, not on your stake. That $4,000 is 4% for the cash buyer, but more than 13% for the one who put in only $30,000.

And every month, part of your loan payment repays the borrowed capital. Your tenant's rent pays for much of it. In other words, your tenant helps you own a property that is less and less burdened by debt.

## What it really changes: your $100,000 can work three times

Let's go back to the investor who has $100,000. He can buy a single apartment in cash and earn $12,000 a year.

He can also put $30,000 into each of three apartments and keep $10,000 in reserve. With the same assumptions, he earns around **$21,800 a year** instead of $12,000. He owns three properties instead of one. And he benefits from rising prices on $300,000 of assets, not $100,000.

That is how a real estate portfolio is built. Not by waiting until you have all the money, but by putting the money you have to work.

## The golden rule

Leverage works as long as the property earns more than the loan costs. That is why the choice of location, developer and a realistic rent is essential. Leverage amplifies the result of a good investment. So you have to start with a good investment.

In Georgia, the ingredients are there: high rents, light taxation, no capital gains tax after two years of ownership and an economy that grew 7.9% in the first half of 2026.
