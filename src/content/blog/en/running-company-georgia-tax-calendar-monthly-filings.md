Setting up a company in Georgia takes a few days. Running it properly, month after month, is another matter. In Switzerland I was used to an annual rhythm: close the accounts, the fiduciary prepares the return, you pay. In Georgia, **business taxation** runs on a monthly cycle. Anyone who doesn't grasp this from the start often finds out through a penalty. Here is the calendar I set up with every client who establishes a business here.

## The 15th of every month: the date that structures everything

In Georgia, the tax year follows the calendar year. But most obligations fall every month, **no later than the 15th of the following month**, on the Revenue Service portal, rs.ge. If the 15th falls on a weekend or public holiday, the deadline generally moves to the next business day ([Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-tax-deadlines-2026-every-rs-ge-filing-date-in-one-calendar)).

What is declared and paid on that date:

- **Tax on distributed profits**, at 15%, for the month in which a distribution took place.
- **Income tax withheld from salaries**, at a flat rate of 20%.
- **Pension contributions**: 2% paid by the employee and 2% by the employer, with the state adding 2% to the employee's account ([Countman](https://countman.ge/en/taxes-in-georgia/)).
- **VAT**, at 18%, for registered companies. The return is monthly, even with no turnover.

What surprises Swiss owners: there are no social charges as we know them at home, no AHV, no Swiss-style occupational pension. The cost of an employee is therefore much easier to read. The monthly discipline, however, is not negotiable.

## VAT: the threshold you cross without noticing

VAT registration becomes mandatory as soon as taxable turnover exceeds **GEL 100,000 over a 12-month period**. You then have to register very quickly, within days ([Countman](https://countman.ge/en/taxes-in-georgia/), [Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-tax-deadlines-2026-every-rs-ge-filing-date-in-one-calendar)).

This is the mistake I see most often in small consulting or service businesses. The threshold is not calculated on the calendar year but on a rolling 12 months. A company starts slowly, signs two good contracts in the autumn and crosses the line without anyone watching the counter. Penalties are then calculated from the date the threshold was crossed. My rule: track rolling turnover every month, from the first lari invoiced.

## The Estonian model day to day: what counts as a distribution

I have already explained how the Estonian model changes the way you steer an SME: the company pays no tax as long as it reinvests its profit. Day to day, this has a consequence many people underestimate.

The Georgian Tax Code does not only tax dividends. It also targets certain transactions treated as distributions: expenses unrelated to the business, goods or services supplied free of charge, and entertainment expenses above a certain cap ([Tax Code, Matsne](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1043717)). In practice, a private car paid by the company, a trip with nothing professional about it or an unsupported invoice can cost 15% in tax, to be declared on the 15th of the following month.

When a dividend is paid, a further 5% withholding applies for an individual shareholder, unless a double tax treaty provides otherwise. That is why I recommend strictly separating the company account from your personal account from day one. What would be simple carelessness in Switzerland becomes a tax here.

## The annual deadlines not to forget

Alongside the monthly rhythm, three dates come back every year:

- **Corporate property tax**: up to 1% of the average book value of taxable assets. The return and payment are due no later than 1 April of the following year, with an advance payment on 15 June ([Andersen](https://ge.andersen.com/georgian-property-tax/)). A company that owns an office or an apartment is concerned.
- **Annual accounts**: every company, even a dormant one, files its financial statements on the accounting and audit supervision portal (SARAS), separate from rs.ge. The usual deadline is 1 October, to be confirmed each year ([Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-tax-deadlines-2026-every-rs-ge-filing-date-in-one-calendar)).
- **Special statuses** (International Company, Virtual Zone, Small Business): they have their own confirmations and conditions, which need to be reviewed every year.

## How I organise it, and what I recommend

My advice comes down to three points.

First, **a local accountant from the first month**, not when the first problem appears. Filings are made in Georgian on rs.ge, and the rules change. A good accountant costs less than a single penalty.

Second, **a monthly dashboard** showing rolling turnover for VAT, the month's distributions and salaries paid. An SME owner should look at the numbers before the tax authority does it for them.

Third, **a strictly business-only company bank account**. It is the best protection against unintended distributions.
