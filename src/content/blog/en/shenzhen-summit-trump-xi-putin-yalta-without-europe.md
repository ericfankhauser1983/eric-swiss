On 19 November in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin meet for the first-ever three-way meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia. No European leader is at the table. In Brussels and Berlin, people are already talking about a new "Yalta". From Tbilisi, I look at this summit differently. The real question is not where Europe is. It is what a deal on Iran, or the lack of one, changes for the routes, the energy and the capital flowing through the Caucasus, and therefore for anyone who wants to **invest in Georgia**.

## What is on the table, and what is not

The White House announced the meeting on 13 November and the Kremlin confirmed it the next day ([CBS News/AFP](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-putin-xi-jinping-meeting-china-us-russia-talks/)). Washington set two topics: reducing nuclear arsenals and the war in Iran. "The president has made it clear that, among other topics, he will discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world," said his spokeswoman Olivia Wales ([SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/trump-xi-putin-meeting/lanttf9lg)).

The nuclear backdrop is heavy. The New START treaty expired on 5 February. The United States and Russia each hold more than 5,000 warheads, China around 500 ([Al-Monitor/Reuters](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/11/analysis-new-yalta-trump-and-putin-push-meeting-xi-wary-hosting)). Trump wants a new treaty that includes Beijing. Beijing refuses, and a senior US official admits: "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement" on that front.

Officially, Ukraine is not on the agenda. The White House rules it out; Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov says the opposite ([HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273717/20261115/china-confirms-talks-first-xi-putin-trump-summit-apec.htm)). The Ukraine negotiation continues on a separate track, in Abu Dhabi.

## "Yalta": a very European reading

The word comes from Kyiv and from Europe. Volodymyr Zelensky fears a "Yalta 2.0" and a "redistribution of spheres of influence". Friedrich Merz, at the EU summit in Dublin, said: "Please, no surprises" ([SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/trump-xi-putin-meeting/lanttf9lg)). The Kremlin, through Dmitry Peskov, rejects the comparison: any agreements "can't be enforced on others".

I understand the European concern. But I also note that the most reluctant of the three is Xi Jinping. According to Reuters, Beijing sees the meeting as "an unpredictable gathering full of risks". China refuses three-way nuclear talks and does not want Ukraine handled in Shenzhen. German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier even reports that Xi told him at the end of October that Europeans "must sit at the table" on Ukraine ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/6/what-a-trump-xi-putin-summit-would-mean-and-what-it-wouldnt)).

In other words, this is not a pre-arranged carve-up of the world. It is a meeting one leader wants for the photo, another for recognition, and the third hosts with suspicion. The real message for Europe lies elsewhere: the issues that affect it directly, energy and the security of its supplies, are now discussed without it.

## Seen from the Gulf and the Caucasus: Iran first

For the Gulf and the Caucasus, the line that matters on the agenda is not nuclear arms, it is Iran. The war began on 28 February. Hormuz remains closed. Only last week, the Revolutionary Guard claimed drone attacks on a US base in Bahrain ([HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273717/20261115/china-confirms-talks-first-xi-putin-trump-summit-apec.htm)). The Gulf states have just decided to set up a forward headquarters for their unified military command in Kuwait ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/10/gcc-agrees-to-set-up-advanced-military-command-headquarters-in-kuwait)). They are not waiting for Brussels.

In the Caucasus, the map is moving too, and fast. On 28 October, Armenia's parliament ratified the framework for the "Trump Route" (TRIPP) by 63 votes to 32. Washington will hold 74% of the joint company for an initial 49 years. On 6 November, the United States, Armenia and Azerbaijan launched the technical working groups ([FinChannel](https://finchannel.com/georgia-and-armenia-at-an-investment-crossroads/141541/opinion/2026/11/)). Meanwhile, trade between Azerbaijan and Georgia reached $888 million in the first nine months of 2026, up 42% ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/business/266687.html)).

Georgia sits in the middle of these worlds: Russia to the north, Iran to the south, China to the east via the Middle Corridor, and now the United States through TRIPP. A deal on Iran in Shenzhen, even a partial one, would be felt here much faster than in Paris or Berlin.

## Three scenarios to keep in mind

- **A signal on Iran.** If Washington, Beijing and Moscow agree on a de-escalation framework, energy prices fall and Hormuz may gradually reopen. Overland routes through the Caucasus lose some urgency but keep their role as a backup. The regional investment climate improves.
- **A photo, nothing more.** This is the most likely scenario if US and Chinese sources are to be believed. The status quo continues: blockade, a closed strait, and strong demand for corridors that bypass Iran, including the one through Georgia.
- **Rising tension.** If the meeting goes badly, pressure on the Gulf rises again. Regional capital then looks harder for diversification, and the Caucasus is part of that.

In all three cases, Georgia remains a transit and diversification country. Its real growth reached 7.5% in 2025 according to Geostat, after 9.7% in 2024 ([FinChannel](https://finchannel.com/georgia-and-armenia-at-an-investment-crossroads/141541/opinion/2026/11/)). The summit will not change its geography. It may change the pace.

## What I take from it as an investor or entrepreneur

1. **Do not read this summit through European glasses only.** "Where is Europe?" is a fair question, but it tells you nothing about your energy costs, your shipping times or where Gulf capital is heading.
2. **Watch the Iran line in the communiqué, if there is one.** It will have the biggest effect on the Caucasus in the coming months.
3. **Build projects that hold up in all three scenarios.** In Georgia, residential property, logistics and business services do not depend on a single outcome.
