On 19 November in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held the first-ever three-way meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia. Around an hour and a half of talks, and no European leader at the table. Before the summit, Brussels and Berlin were talking about a new "Yalta". From Tbilisi, I mostly watched for one line: the one on Iran. There isn't one. And for anyone who wants to **invest in Georgia**, that silence says more than the rest of the communiqué.

## What the joint statement says

The text, released by the Kremlin and then by the White House, comes down to a few commitments ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-xi-putin-china-apec-summit/)). The three leaders "reaffirmed their commitment to maintaining strategic stability and international security", restated that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and pledged a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. With no timetable and no negotiating framework ([Alhurra](https://alhurra.com/en/56048)).

The context makes that commitment modest. The New START treaty expired on 5 February. The United States and Russia each hold more than 5,000 warheads, China around 600. Xi Jinping committed to no disarmament talks ([RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/trump-xi-putin-summit-four-key-takeaways/33902100.html)).

The rest of the text is more surprising: a pledge to increase the supply of oil, gas and refined products "for the benefit of all countries", a call to reform the UN Security Council, and a warning against abuses by the International Criminal Court.

Donald Trump spoke of "making history". Xi Jinping, according to Xinhua, of a "positive, constructive and fruitful" meeting. Most analysts mainly note that no concrete breakthrough came out of it.

## The "Yalta" did not happen

The word came from Kyiv and from Europe. Volodymyr Zelensky feared a "Yalta 2.0"; Friedrich Merz had said before the summit: "Please, no surprises" ([SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/trump-xi-putin-meeting/lanttf9lg)). There were none. Ukraine does not appear in the statement. After the meeting, Vladimir Putin even hardened his tone: no negotiations, he said, after the attacks on the Crimean bridge ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-xi-putin-china-apec-summit/)).

I do not conclude that Europe can relax. There was no carve-up of the world, but the message is the same: the issues that affect Europe directly, energy and the security of its supplies, are now discussed without it. And China, the most reluctant of the three, conceded nothing.

## Seen from the Gulf and the Caucasus: the silence on Iran

For the Gulf and the Caucasus, the line that mattered was the one on Iran. Neither Iran nor the Strait of Hormuz appears in the statement. Yet Donald Trump had raised both with Xi Jinping just before, in their bilateral meeting: "Both the United States and China have an interest in ensuring that energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz, and that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon" ([Alhurra](https://alhurra.com/en/56048)). Neither Xi nor Putin mentioned the war publicly.

The timing is telling. The day before the summit, on 18 November, Iran launched drone and missile strikes on Bahrain and Kuwait, the first in roughly two months ([RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/trump-xi-putin-summit-four-key-takeaways/33902100.html)). China has already adapted: the Gulf's share of its seaborne oil imports fell from 52% last year to 34% in September ([Alhurra](https://alhurra.com/en/56048)). Beijing is not waiting for Hormuz to reopen. It is diversifying.

In the Caucasus, the map was already moving before Shenzhen. On 28 October, Armenia's parliament ratified the framework for the "Trump Route" (TRIPP) by 63 votes to 32, with Washington holding 74% of the joint company for 49 years ([FinChannel](https://finchannel.com/georgia-and-armenia-at-an-investment-crossroads/141541/opinion/2026/11/)). Trade between Azerbaijan and Georgia reached $888 million over nine months, up 42% ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/business/266687.html)).

## The scenario that played out: a photo, nothing more

Before the summit, I saw three scenarios: a signal on Iran, a mere photo, or rising tension. The second one played out, with a hint of the third, since the strikes of 18 November were a reminder that the Gulf remains exposed.

In practice, the status quo continues: blockade, a closed strait, and strong demand for the routes that bypass Iran, including the one through Georgia (the Baku–Tbilisi–Kars railway and the ports of Poti and Batumi). Gulf capital, for its part, has one more reason to diversify.

Georgia remains a transit and diversification country. Its real growth reached 7.5% in 2025 according to Geostat, after 9.7% in 2024 ([FinChannel](https://finchannel.com/georgia-and-armenia-at-an-investment-crossroads/141541/opinion/2026/11/)). Shenzhen did not change its geography. It confirmed that nobody in Washington, Beijing or Moscow is in a hurry to settle the Iran question.

## What I take from it as an investor or entrepreneur

1. **Do not read this summit through European glasses only.** "Where was Europe?" is a fair question, but it tells you nothing about your energy costs, your shipping times or where Gulf capital is heading.
2. **Take the silence on Iran seriously.** It means the Hormuz crisis can last. Work out your energy and freight exposure on that assumption.
3. **Build projects that hold up without a quick resolution.** In Georgia, residential property, logistics and business services actually benefit from this status quo.
