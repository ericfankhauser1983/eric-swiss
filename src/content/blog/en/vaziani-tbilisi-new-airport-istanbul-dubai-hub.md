I started my working life at 15, as an apprentice at Swissair. At 18, the airline sent me to New York for a year. I was there on 11 September 2001. A few weeks later, Swissair was grounded. I kept one conviction from it: an airport is only worth what connects to it. That is the lens through which I read the project for Tbilisi's new airport, and what it could change for anyone looking to **invest in Georgia**.

## The Vaziani project in a few figures

The Georgian government wants to build a new airport on the former Vaziani military base, about twenty kilometres east of Tbilisi, between the capital and Rustavi. The figures reported by FFI:

- **Target capacity:** 20 million passengers a year, about four times the current airport.
- **Estimated cost:** about $1.3 billion.
- **Potential operator:** Changi Airports International, the operator of Singapore's airport, confirmed in September its interest in operating and investing in it. It is not a contract yet.
- **Feasibility study:** awarded to the Berlin firm amd.sigma, under a tender backed by the Asian Development Bank.
- **Timeline:** it has slipped several times. The latest date mentioned is an opening in 2032.

In the meantime, the current airport is saturated. It handled a record 5.4 million passengers in 2025, for a nominal capacity of 5 million. Works worth $150 million, launched in July 2026, are due to raise its capacity to 10 million by 2028.

## What Istanbul brought to Turkey

Istanbul Airport opened at the end of 2018. In 2025, it handled **84 million passengers**, 48% of them long-haul transfers, to more than 330 destinations ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/istanbuls-airports-set-all-time-passenger-records-in-2025-217313)).

But the real engine is Turkish Airlines. In 2025, the airline carried a record **92.6 million passengers**, with 516 aircraft, to 356 destinations in 132 countries. It is aiming for 813 aircraft in 2033 ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/business/transportation/turkish-airlines-flew-record-926-million-passengers-in-2025)). According to the airline, no other carrier serves as many countries.

The lesson is simple: Istanbul did not become a hub because someone built a big terminal. It became one because an ambitious national airline turned every flight into a connection to the rest of the world. Tourism, real estate and business followed.

## What Dubai is preparing with Al Maktoum

Dubai followed the same strategy, with Emirates. In the 2025-26 financial year, the airline carried 53.2 million passengers to 152 cities in 80 countries. The group posted a record profit of $6.6 billion, despite the war in the region ([Emirates](https://www.emirates.com/media-centre/emirates-group-achieves-record-profit-of-aed-244-bn-us-66-bn-in-2025-26/)).

And Dubai is already looking further ahead. The new Al Maktoum airport, at $35 billion, is eventually meant to handle 260 million passengers a year. A second runway is planned for 2027, and the first phase is due to open in 2032 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business-technology-review/al-maktoum-international-accelerates-toward-worlds-biggest-airport-status)). Dubai did not wait to be saturated before building. It builds ahead.

## Georgia has the location, it lacks the airline

Georgia has an asset many envy: its position. Tbilisi lies on the shortest route between Europe and Central Asia, a few hours' flight from London, Dubai, Delhi or Almaty.

It has a second, often forgotten asset: its visa regime. Citizens of 94 countries can enter without a visa and stay for up to a year ([OC Media](https://oc-media.org/georgia-tightens-entry-rules-for-citizens-of-17-countries)). Holders of a valid visa or residence permit from the United States, the United Kingdom or the Schengen area can generally stay up to 90 days in any 180 ([Madloba](https://madloba.info/en/faq/visa-and-border/who-needs-a-visa-for-georgia/)). For many nationalities, Tbilisi is one of the few open doors between East and West. That is exactly the profile of a transit hub.

What is missing is a major airline based there. Over the first nine months of 2025, the leading carrier at Georgian airports was Wizz Air, with about 1.07 million passengers. Georgian Airways had about 509,000, and Turkish Airlines 458,000 ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/passenger-traffic-at-georgian-airports-reaches-record-6-5-million-in-2025/)). We are a very long way from the Istanbul or Dubai model.

At Swissair, I learned that a hub rests on three pillars: an airport, an airline and a network. Vaziani can provide the first. Changi's possible arrival would bring world-class expertise. The rest will depend on Georgia's ability to attract an airline that turns Tbilisi into a real connecting platform.

## What it changes to invest in Georgia

I remain cautious. The timeline has already slipped, and 2032 is a long way off. I never buy a property on the promise of infrastructure alone. But some effects are already visible:

- **Air traffic is growing every year.** Over the first nine months of 2025, it rose by 14%, and by 32% in Batumi.
- **The expansion of the current airport** is due to double its capacity by 2028. That means more visitors, and therefore more demand for short-term rentals in Tbilisi.
- **The east of Tbilisi**, between the city and Rustavi, deserves close watching for the long term.

When I look at Istanbul and Dubai, I take away one thing: cities that become air crossroads change scale within a generation. Georgia is not there yet. But it has the location, the openness and now a project. This is the time to understand the market, not to wait until everyone else has understood it.
